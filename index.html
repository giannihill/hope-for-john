John’s story

I was born fighting.

I was born two months early and weighed a little over two pounds. Back in 1974, that was a medical challenge — and it put my life in danger. My love of food probably saved me: they tried feeding me through tubes, but I pulled them out and took to the bottle immediately. Clearly, I had other ideas. I still love food. I’m what you’d call a foodie, 100%, and I love to cook. That’s part of the problem now, because I can’t enjoy all the foods I like — I have to limit a lot of them. You see, I have End-Stage Renal Disease, and technically I’m supposed to follow the renal diet. Admittedly, I try, but it’s difficult. I’m also limited to 32 ounces of fluid a day, which is hard when I stay active — walking almost every day and getting to the gym when I can. Summers are difficult, too, with the heat and the fluid restriction. This is my reality — but there are thousands of others experiencing these challenges, and they also need help finding a kidney donor.

You’re probably wondering how I ended up here. Over a decade ago, extraordinarily high blood pressure damaged my kidneys. The problem progressed, and in May 2024 my life changed forever: I lost most of the function of my kidneys and was diagnosed with End-Stage Renal Disease. Essentially, my kidneys lost the ability to adequately filter waste and fluids from my blood, along with other life-sustaining functions. That’s when my dialysis treatments began — three times a week, three and a half hours each time — and my journey of finding a healthy kidney through donation began, too.

“The most important thing I enjoy is being a parent… I want to be around for him, and hopefully for grandchildren someday.”

— John

A little bit about me: I enjoy walking, tabletop games, watching a variety of TV shows and movies, reading, cooking, music, languages, playing basketball, and traveling — though traveling takes planning to schedule dialysis along the way. The most important thing I enjoy is being a parent. I’m a proud father to my son Benjamin, who serves in the United States Coast Guard. I’ve always wanted to be a father, and it brings me immense joy. Finding a kidney would give me a future — with the restored freedom and health I lost when I was diagnosed.

I can only accomplish this with your help. Whether it’s by donating a kidney, spreading the word, or giving to my cause, your assistance is vital. I appreciate anything you can do — and I thank you in advance.

Questions people usually ask

Do I have to be a match to donate?

No. Thanks to paired exchange programs, you don’t have to be compatible with John — your donation can still lead to him receiving a kidney. The transplant team can explain exactly how it works.

What is the screening process like?

It starts with a simple registration form through UPMC Hamot (linked above). From there, the transplant team guides you through health questions and testing. Everything is confidential, and there’s no obligation — you can pause or stop at any point.

Who can be a living donor?

Living-donor programs generally look for donors between 18 and 69 who are in good health — no diabetes, high blood pressure, obesity, or cardiac issues, no active substance abuse, and a solid support system. The transplant team makes the final call, and they’re glad to answer questions before you commit to anything.

Does donating cost me anything?

The recipient’s insurance covers the donor’s surgery and hospital stay. For other costs like travel and lost wages, the National Living Donor Assistance Center (NLDAC) may be able to help. Ask the transplant team — they walk every donor through this.

Why is living donation such a big deal?

Living donation can happen in months rather than years. Kidneys from living donors typically function better and last longer, surgery can be scheduled in advance, and for John it would mean the end of dialysis and its restrictions.

What if I can’t donate — how else can I help?

You can still change the odds. Share this page, talk about John with people who might be able to donate, and register as an organ donor. For thousands of patients, word of mouth is exactly how their donor found them.

A note on John’s privacy. To protect John, please don’t post his personal information anywhere. All donor questions and offers should go directly to the UPMC Hamot transplant team — they coordinate everything confidentially.
